Module: Trustworthy generative AI. Session 2 (18 November), 13:30 to 18:00.

In TD 1, you measured five models separately and placed each one in the portfolio. Your TD 1 outputs (traces-td1.csv, reference-td1.csv and your summary tables) are the raw material for this session: we do not re-open TD 1 and no new batch of network calls is launched. This session builds the two decisions of a service: having several models vote when the answer carries consequences, and assigning each question to the cheapest model that remains up to it. By 18:00, your vote runs on the twenty questions (comparing the 2-of-3 panel and the 3-of-5 quorum on your traces), you have explained and isolated a wrong-consensus case in SQL, and your routing rule is written and exercised on a simulator.

What you can do by the end of the session

  • Implement a k-of-n vote on a panel of distinct families, with a strict majority, which keeps a value or abstains without manufacturing an answer, and measure the difference between a 2-of-3 quorum and a 3-of-5 quorum on your own traces.
  • Identify a wrong consensus in traces, isolate the faulty SQL clause and write a diagnostic query on data/boutique.sqlite that extracts the exact rows responsible for the gap with the reference.
  • Write a routing rule per question family, locked to the exact accuracy rates observed in TD 1, which provides for the quorum, the deferral and the abstention.
  • Read the decisions of a resource simulator, explain the priority order of its code and adapt its policy to your rule.

Step 1 - The k-of-n vote (Pair A)

Complete Orchestrateur.vote_k_sur_n(self, traces_question, k) in skeleton-multi-llm-en.py. Each element of traces_question carries provider, model_requested, value (an integer in cents, or None) and verdict (executed or error). The contract:

  1. only the executed proposals with a value vote; an error or an unavailability stays in n without a vote;
  2. k is a strict majority: n // 2 < k <= n, otherwise the method refuses with ValueError;
  3. two proposals from the same provider and model pair are refused with ValueError: a duplicate is not a second vote;
  4. if a value reaches k votes, the method returns {"consensus": True, "value": v, "votes": nombre, "models": [...]};
  5. otherwise it returns {"consensus": False, "value": None, "votes": plus grand nombre de voix, "models": []}. It never invents a value.

Exercise the vote:

python3 atelier-vote.py                                   # four synthetic examples, no network
python3 atelier-vote.py --traces traces-td1.csv --case C02 # the real traces of one question

The second call takes, for each member of the fixed three-model panel, its last attempt on the question, decides, then compares the value kept with your reference. The comparison comes after the decision: the vote does not know the reference.

Comparing 2-of-3 against 3-of-5 on your TD 1 traces: Run the evaluation across all twenty questions (C01 to C20) and build a comparison table in reports/td2.md showing side by side, for every question:

  1. the decision of the fixed 2-of-3 panel (openai/gpt-oss-120b, cohere/north-mini-code:free, qwen/qwen3.8-27b, threshold k = 2);
  2. the decision of a 3-of-5 quorum across all five models of your traces-td1.csv (threshold k = 3, computed by your vote_k_sur_n method);
  3. agreement with your reference value (reference-td1.csv) and an explicit explanation for every question where moving from 2-of-3 to 3-of-5 changes the decision (for instance turning an abstention into a consensus, or shifting the vote margin because of an HTTP 429 or an invalid SQL statement), citing the exact values and verdicts from your traces.

Use only your stored TD 1 traces: no new network batch is needed.

Deliverable of the step: the completed method, and the commented twenty-question comparison table (2-of-3 versus 3-of-5).

Step 2 - The wrong consensus (Pair B)

Search all your TD 1 traces (across all five models queried in TD 1, not only the three models of the fixed panel) for an identified case where at least two models return the same wrong value (verdict = incorrect with identical value). Declare the panel and threshold k out of n, then show whether that shared wrong value wins the fixed 2-of-3 panel, whether it wins the 3-of-5 quorum, or whether it is outvoted. Reminder: dots-studio/dots-3-note-preview:free and openai/gpt-oss-20b belong to the five TD 1 models, but Dots and 20B are not members of the fixed three-model panel of atelier-vote.py.

Your analysis in reports/td2.md is carried out on an SQL case executed on data/boutique.sqlite:

  • either a shared wrong value found in your own traces-td1.csv;
  • or, if your 100 TD 1 cells contain no shared wrong value between any two models, the historical case DEMO-SQL-C11 below. Its absence from your batch does not cap the mark.

The DEMO-VOTE example below illustrates the mechanism on a one-row toy table; it explains the date boundary and is not an analysis case.

In either case, provide these three pieces of evidence:

  1. Identification and quorum verdict: question identifier, models agreeing on the wrong value, shared wrong value, independent reference value from your reference-td1.csv, and what the 2-of-3 and 3-of-5 quorums return.
  2. Faulty SQL clause diagnosis: the two complete SQL queries and the exact explanation of the WHERE or JOIN condition that produces the wrong value.
  3. Delta diagnostic SQL query on data/boutique.sqlite: write and execute a SELECT query on data/boutique.sqlite that lists the exact customer_id rows (and their order revenue excluding VAT) counted by your reference query but missed (or wrongly added) by the faulty queries, accounting for the exact difference in cents between the wrong value and your reference.

Mechanism illustrated: DEMO-VOTE

The workshop's wrong consensus example has proposals [0, 0, 100], reference 100 cents and threshold 2 of 3. To explain a possible shared SQL cause, use this invented sale: date 2026-01-01, amount 100 cents. The requested period is 1 January inclusive to 2 January exclusive. Two proposals use sale_date > '2026-01-01'; the third uses sale_date >= '2026-01-01'. All use sale_date < '2026-01-02' and COALESCE(SUM(amount_cents), 0). Execute both variants on this one-row toy table, explain the boundary and compare the quorum with the independent reference. These values and queries are constructed teaching material, not model outputs or answers on the shop database.

Historical shop-database case: DEMO-SQL-C11

On question C11 (total revenue excluding VAT, in euro cents, generated by customers who are exactly 25 years old on the reference date 2026-12-01, over all their orders), two distinct TD 1 models, openai/gpt-oss-20b (Groq) and cohere/north-mini-code:free (OpenRouter), returned two different SQLite queries during the September 2026 pilot batch. Both run without error on data/boutique.sqlite and agree on the same wrong value 0. Queries reproduced as returned by the models:

-- Query M1, openai/gpt-oss-20b (returns 0 on data/boutique.sqlite)
SELECT COALESCE(SUM(o.quantity_product * p.price), 0) AS revenue_cents
FROM orders o
JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
JOIN product p ON o.product_id = p.product_id
WHERE c.birth_date = '2001-12-01';

-- Query M2, cohere/north-mini-code:free (returns 0 on data/boutique.sqlite)
SELECT COALESCE(SUM(o.quantity_product * p.price), 0) AS revenue_cents
FROM customers c
JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
JOIN product p ON o.product_id = p.product_id
WHERE CAST(strftime('%Y-%m-%d', '2026-12-01') AS TEXT) >= c.birth_date
  AND CAST(strftime('%Y-%m-%d', '2026-12-01', '+1 year') AS TEXT) <= c.birth_date;

If your TD 1 batch has no shared wrong value (or alongside your own case), run M1 and M2 on data/boutique.sqlite, compare their shared output (0) with your reference value for C11 in reference-td1.csv, and explain which birth-date window each WHERE condition actually tests, against the window required by "exactly 25 years old on 2026-12-01". Then write the diagnostic SQL query on data/boutique.sqlite that lists the customers aged 25 on 2026-12-01 and the revenue of their orders: the sum of these amounts must match your reference, which accounts for the entire gap with 0.

Deliverable: one explained case with its identifier, source/nature, agreeing models, reference, declared 2-of-3 and 3-of-5 quorum results, SQL root cause, executed delta query on data/boutique.sqlite and independent checking method.

Step 3 - The routing rule (Pair C)

Write, on one page, the rule of the sub-group. For each question family (F1 period, F2 completed age at a date, F2b age at the order, F3 product list, and combinations):

  • the exact TD 1 rate: cite the cells of your TD 1 table (C01 to C20) belonging to the family and the correct / cases ratio observed for your models on that family;
  • the first model called: the cheapest one, according to the portfolio, among those that your TD 1 table shows to be correct on the family;
  • the checking: none, 2-of-3 quorum, 3-of-5 quorum, reference, review, according to what an error costs for three uses (internal dashboard, figure shown to the customer, amount paid out). Consistency constraint: calling a single free model with no check (none) is only admissible for a low-stakes internal dashboard AND on a family where that model achieved 100 % accuracy in your TD 1 table; whenever a model made at least one error on the family in TD 1, or whenever the figure is shown to a customer or governs a payout, the rule must require a quorum or a deterministic check;
  • the course of action if the first model is unavailable: next model, waiting for the refill when the request can wait, abstention.

Keep simple period and product questions: they help compare routing choices. Twenty questions support a batch-specific hypothesis, not a general error rate, a permanent ranking or a quorum error guarantee.

Then launch the simulator:

python3 atelier-routage.py                       # the seven scenarios
python3 atelier-routage.py --scenario deferrable # one scenario

Read each decision (run, defer, abstain), its resource and its reason. Then adapt RESOURCES and choose to your rule: replace or extend the resources with your portfolio models, their eligibility level derived from TD 1 and their price, and include the justifying TD 1 cells in the "reason" string of every decision. Finally, explain in reports/td2.md why, in the supplied choose function, an immediately available subscription resource (cash == 0, allocation > 0) is selected before deferring (defer) to wait for an exhausted free resource (cash == 0, allocation == 0) whose refill occurs before deadline_minutes, and under what operational condition that priority should be reversed.

Deliverable of the step: the one-page rule, the simulator outputs before and after adaptation, and the analysis of the priority order in choose.
