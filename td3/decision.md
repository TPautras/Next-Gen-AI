## Named use case: a revenue figure shown to the customer

The assistant answers a merchant who asks, in the seller dashboard of a marketplace, "what did I sell to 25-year-old customers between March and June?". The figure is displayed to that merchant as is.

**What an error costs.** A wrong figure shown to a customer leads to a support ticket, a correction to publish and lost trust. Assumption, written down and not measured: **about 50 EUR per wrong figure** (support time plus goodwill gesture). An abstention sends the question to an analyst. Assumption: **about 5 EUR per abstention** (a few minutes of human work).

**Frequencies observed in this batch** (twenty questions, one draw at temperature 0):

- gpt-oss-20b alone: 3 wrong out of 20, all on age questions (C02, C08, C11). The expected error cost is about 3/20 × 50 = 7.5 EUR per question, against 0.00013 USD of API equivalent.
- gpt-oss-120b alone: 0 wrong out of 20. But a single model gives no signal when it is wrong. In this batch two other models agreed on a wrong age value (C10, `td2/wrong-consensus.md`).
- Routing + 2-of-3 on age: 0 wrong, 2 abstentions out of 20 (C10, C17), so about 2/20 × 5 = 0.5 EUR per question of escalation.

**Decision: routing rule + 2-of-3 quorum on every question with an age filter** (`td2/routing-rule.md`):

- period and product questions go to gpt-oss-20b alone (all five models were correct on these ten cells);
- age questions go to the 2-of-3 panel of distinct families;
- an abstention goes to a human.

The model cost is negligible next to the cost of an error, by about four orders of magnitude, so the choice is made on the error, not on the token price. The cost per question of this configuration is **N/A**: north-mini-code has no paid comparator. The known subtotal is 0.014999 USD on 29 of 43 attempts. That is not a complete total, so it is not projected to a yearly bill. Real spend is 0 on the free routes used.

For comparison only, the configurations that are fully quantified project as follows for 48,000 requests a year (`methode-cout-en.md` step 5): routing (dashboard) 0.000194 × 48,000 ≈ 9.3 USD; gpt-oss-120b alone 0.000250 × 48,000 ≈ 12.0 USD. These are API equivalents under the batch's mean token envelope, not invoices. A complete projection for the recommended configuration needs one of two things: a dated paid price for north-mini-code, or replacing it in the panel with a priced model of another family and measuring it.

For the other two uses: the **internal dashboard** takes the routing rule alone (0.000194 USD per question, 20/20 here). An **amount paid out** takes the 3-of-5 quorum, the boundary fixtures and human review. The 3-of-5 vote abstained on 4 of 20 questions here, and that is the intended behaviour.

## Limits

- **Twenty questions** on one small base, one draw per cell at temperature 0. They establish neither a general error rate, nor a permanent ranking, nor an error bound guaranteed by quorum.
- **Coverage.** Only three families and their combinations, all in one SQL dialect. Untested: returns, VAT, other tables, wording variations, longer prompts, other languages.
- **The rule is fitted on the same twenty results** it is evaluated on. It needs a fresh sample.
- **One account per provider.** One Groq account ran batches A, B and D, and one OpenRouter account ran C and E. The per-minute limits we hit (Qwen output tokens per minute 1000) are those of that organisation on that day.
- **Simulator assumptions** (`td2/routage-adapte.py`): availability and reset times are scenario inputs; the question family is assumed known before routing; cash is the mean API equivalent of this batch.
- **Cost assumptions.** Plain text, no cache, no tools, no volume discount. The two error costs (50 EUR and 5 EUR) are assumptions. Human review is not costed in the configuration columns.
- **Preview and free routes** (dots-3-note-preview, `:free` models) can change or disappear without notice.
